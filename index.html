From AI launch to AI habit

Factorial has rebuilt its platform around AI agents, and part of its new funding is tied to the value it creates for customers. That only works if small companies actually use the AI every week. This kit is how I would get them there, prove the hours saved, and keep support from becoming the bottleneck.

Describe the customer and the plan builds itself. Each week adds one step up: first employees ask, then they act, then managers, then HR's heaviest work. The tasks are use cases to switch on where the product supports them.

4. Support triage

Public reviews of Factorial point to the same support gaps: slow answers after onboarding, bugs bounced between teams, billing surprises and slow data exports. Paste a customer message and see where it should go. This is a simple keyword router to show the routing rules, not a trained model.

5. The support model behind it

Three levels, so the AI answers what it can, people handle what it can't, and nothing goes quiet.

Level 0

Assistant and help centre

How-to questions answered in the product, in the customer's language.

Every unanswered question is logged and becomes a help article or a policy gap to fix.

Level 1

Support specialists by market

Configuration, access and first look at bugs. Owns the ticket until it is closed, even when another team is working on it.

No ticket closed without the customer confirming the fix.

Level 2

Specialists and engineering

Payroll and compliance experts, billing, data requests and engineering, with a reproducible bug report every time.
